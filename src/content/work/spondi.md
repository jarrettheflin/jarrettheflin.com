Founded at MIT, Spondi was my primary project, which I built through workshops,
labs, clubs, and hackathons. It grew out of my own diagnosis with ankylosing
spondylitis (AS), an autoimmune arthritis of the spine.

It started as research. My team and I met with and collected data from hundreds
of AS patients, many of them on IL-17 and JAK inhibitors, and used LLMs to sort
and code their care journeys into patterns. I also talked with physicians,
nutritionists, occupational therapists, physical therapists, coaches, and
community leaders to map the care available beyond the clinic. That became a
first-of-its-kind partnership with AS-specific nutritionists, coaches, and
therapists, and a guide to help each patient find the resources relevant to them.

In 2025, I recruited a team of MIT and Harvard students, from multimodal
engineers to health policy specialists, with technical advisors from Amazon Alexa
and Moderna, to design and run an alpha trial. Spondi replaced form-based
checklists with a voice check-in. You talk about your day the way you would to a
friend ("I felt good after yoga but stiff after sitting in the car"), and a set
of agents sorts the conversation by category and time, turning a messy voice log
into a structured timeline. From there it connected activity, diet and stress to
symptom trends, suggested small changes, and wrote summaries to bring to a
doctor's visit.

The first version followed a rigid, hard-coded script, and transcription lag made
each check-in feel like a checklist read aloud. We rebuilt it as a modular
conversation and tightened the timing across the transcription pipeline until
replies felt immediate. Voice logs were pseudonymized and encrypted in transit
and at rest.

The marketing taught us as much as the product. Our first ads led with "MIT
students advancing AS research," and patients pushed back, worried their data
would be sold or seen by insurers. We switched to a private, effortless symptom
log and put the encryption and privacy policy up front. The waitlist campaigns
came in at $4.38 per applicant, and 113 alpha users sent daily voice check-ins
and in-app feedback. The trial proved the core idea: talking is easier than
filling out a form, so people logged more consistently, and consistent logs are
what turn into useful insights.

Spondi's voice check-ins became the core of [immune.fit](https://www.immune.fit/).
